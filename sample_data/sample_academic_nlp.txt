Quantitative stylometry operates on the empirical hypothesis that individual authors possess characteristic linguistic habits that manifest consistently across written corpora. These stylistic habits, termed idiolects or stylistic fingerprints, encompass lexical selection distributions, syntactic parse tree depths, clause coordination patterns, and punctuation frequencies. While earlier computational methods relied predominantly on surface-level statistics such as word length and vocabulary richness indices, modern frameworks integrate multi-dimensional token distributions with vector space representations. TF-IDF vectorization and character n-gram analyses provide robust discriminative representations across varying genre contexts. However, stylistic measurements remain sensitive to sample length variance and domain shifts. Consequently, empirical cross-validation must be maintained to mitigate confounding stylistic variables before drawing definitive taxonomic conclusions.
